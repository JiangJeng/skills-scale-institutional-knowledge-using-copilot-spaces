# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This folder contains the OctoAcme project management process documentation used to guide project planning, execution, communication, release, and continuous improvement.

## Project management process overview

OctoAcme follows a lightweight but disciplined project lifecycle that starts with initiation and ends with retrospective learning. At the beginning of a project, teams validate the business need, identify stakeholders, define success metrics, and create a simple one-page project charter with a rough timeline and initial risks. This helps ensure the work is justified, prioritized, and aligned with stakeholder expectations before moving into detailed planning.

Once a project is approved, the planning phase turns the idea into an actionable backlog. Teams run kickoff sessions, prioritize work with clear acceptance criteria, estimate effort, identify dependencies, agree on milestones, and define the Definition of Done. This creates a shared understanding of scope and quality and helps the team break the work into small, shippable increments. Planning also captures ownership and risk early so that issues can be managed before they become blockers.

During execution, OctoAcme emphasizes a regular team rhythm with standups, weekly delivery syncs, demos, and issue tracking through a project board. Product managers define outcomes, project managers coordinate schedules and communication, and developers deliver work with quality gates such as tests, code review, and CI verification. Risk management and stakeholder communication are integrated into this process through a risk register, escalation paths, and regular status updates. This keeps the team informed, reduces surprises, and enables fast decision-making when issues arise.

OctoAcme also uses structured release and deployment practices to reduce operational risk and improve observability. Before production release, teams confirm that acceptance criteria are met, CI and security checks pass, release notes are prepared, and rollback plans are documented. After each release or milestone, the team holds a retrospective to capture wins, identify improvement opportunities, and assign action items. This continuous improvement loop ensures the process evolves based on real delivery experience and team learning.
